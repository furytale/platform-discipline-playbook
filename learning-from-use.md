# Learning from use

[Back to the Playbook](README.md) · [Core](core.md) · [Working note](core.md#working-note)

This playbook grew from practice. Using it in new work should reveal where its guidance travels well, where a boundary needs to be narrower, and where a rule adds effort without improving the decision.

This is not a compliance score for a team. It is a short record of what the playbook changed—or failed to change—in one real situation. Do not create one for every routine question.

## Before the work

Record only enough context to understand the later result:

- the decision or learning goal;
- why the question matters now;
- the setting: startup, product company, or outsourcing / presales;
- the part of the playbook being used;
- the existing path or baseline, if known;
- the expected effort and review point.

## When the question closes

Record:

- the observation or existing basis that mattered;
- whether the work confirmed, narrowed, changed, deferred, or stopped the proposed action;
- the decision made, or the decision that remained pending;
- which tasks, estimates, requirements, or commitments changed;
- active effort, waiting, coordination, and material rework where these are available;
- what helped, if identifiable, and what created confusion or unnecessary work, if anything.

Missing information stays missing. Do not invent a baseline, saving, or prevented failure after the fact.

## Later, if the result can be observed

Return only when someone has access and responsibility to observe the action. Record the relevant signal, the conditions, and whether a material assumption held. A finite engagement does not become open-ended monitoring because this page exists.

## How a case can change the playbook

A serious one-off failure can justify a targeted safeguard. Repeated friction across different cases may justify changing a rule or example. A preference for different wording may justify an edit without becoming a methodological claim.

Keep three things separate:

- what participants observed;
- what they reported about using the playbook;
- what the maintainer infers from the case.

One successful decision does not show that the playbook caused the later outcome. One awkward use does not show that the underlying rule is wrong. State the context, make the smallest justified change, and keep contrary cases visible.
